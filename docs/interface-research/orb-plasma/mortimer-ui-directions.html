Findings, ranked by impact

  1. High
    Copy, Share and Export are unreadable on the second screen. They should be enabled (they disable only for protected results), and the app never pins dark appearance, so system buttons may be drawing in light mode. screenshotcause untestedWorkspaceResultPane.swift:129, :144 · no preferredColorScheme in app source
  2. High
    The conversation never says what Mortimer is doing right now. "Thinking" lives only in the orb label; the thread has no working, tool or agent row. codeOrbFieldView.swift:364 · ConversationThreadView.swift
  3. High
    Approvals are off-screen. A pending confirmation shows as amber text "Confirmation needed in Output" and a dot on the Panels button; you must open the drawer to act. codeOrbFieldView.swift:239 · TopBarView.swift:190
  4. High
    There is no way to type a request. The only text field addressed to Mortimer appears after you stage an attachment. codeAttachmentTrayView.swift:63-65
  5. High
    Titles repeat. The tile draws the title, then the result pane draws it again; the display header says "Supporting display" and "SUPPORTING DISPLAY" on one line. code + screenshotDisplayWindowView.swift:172-176, :225 · WorkspaceResultPane.swift:33
  6. Medium
    Results waste the screen and the controls crowd them. Tiles are forced tall around one line of text, and each carries six text controls. screenshotDisplayWindowView.swift:260 · WorkspaceResultPane.swift:55-63
  7. Medium
    State colours don't form one language. The tokens say amber means attention, but the assistant's orb channel is orange #E07020 and "listening" is green in the label. codeAppTheme.swift:23 · AppTuning.swift:126 · OrbFieldView.swift:366-367
  8. Medium
    Keyboard and VoiceOver gaps. Space is captured for push-to-talk, the app has one keyboard shortcut, and the top bar and mic controls carry no accessibility labels. codebehaviour untestedMicControlsView.swift:100-108 · MortimerHostApp.swift:276

Five more lower-impact findings (empty state, raw error banner, 44 text sizes at 10 pt or smaller, token drift, discoverability) are in the deep review doc.

Worth keeping

Strong already

  1. One glass modifier with opaque fallbacks for Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast (Glass.swift).
  2. Measured voice levels; it says "Audio level unavailable" instead of faking motion.
  3. Results land as quiet cards with an "N new" pill rather than stealing the stage.
  4. Content is never sent without an explicit approval.
  5. Voice and pointer share one action dispatcher.

DIRECTION 5 · YOUR SUGGESTION

Result Carousel

Results become glass panels on a carousel you turn through by swipe, arrow key or voice. Below are both placements you raised: in the open area beside the conversation, and as an overlay over the whole screen.

How it turns
Two-finger swipe moves one panel; ← → rotate, P pins, Delete closes, Esc leaves B. Voice "next", "previous" and "show the weather one" already exist as actions (result_next, result_previous, ConsoleActionCoordinator.swift:432-435). Motion 360 ms; Reduce Motion cross-fades the front panel only.
How many stack
History keeps up to 20 results (AppTuning.swift:44). A carousel reads well at 3 to 7, so it should hold pinned plus the 10 recent the app already uses (WorkspaceRecents.swift:16), with older ones left in the Results menu.
Ends, not a loop
Next and previous stop at the ends today (WorkspaceStore.swift:509-516), so both mockups show nothing past result 1. Making it wrap is a change to accepted behaviour; your call.
Approvals and dismissal
Approvals never ride the carousel, where turning could hide them. They sit in a fixed amber strip, like today's attachment approval (AttachmentTrayView.swift:28-30). Close and pin live only on the front panel.
Readability · accessibility
Neighbour titles stay sharp; only their bodies blur. With Reduce Transparency or Increase Contrast, Glass.swift drops to opaque fills, so neighbours become flat dimmed slabs; that version needs drawing too. VoiceOver sees one list, "Results, 2 of 5, Weather tomorrow", with only the front panel exposed.
Effort · risk
A: M, 4 to 5 weeks. B: M, 5 to 6 weeks (modal layer, focus trap, voice pill). Untested: whether Liquid Glass renders correctly on a 3D-rotated layer, and whether B's 72% scrim keeps text legible over the live console. Both need a spike first.

Placement pick: A, with B as "show all results"

This is a voice-first app, and B covers the orb and conversation every time an answer lands; the current layout deliberately keeps voice state beside results. A's side panels also sit over a quiet background, which avoids the glass-over-busy-content problem B depends on a scrim to solve. B is the better way to review several results at once, so it earns a place as a summoned view ("show all my results"), not as where answers land.

A costs width: its 620-pixel panel clears the app's 480-point minimum result width, but Composed Answers has to stack its blocks in one column there. Compare mode still needs two results side by side, so it stays its own view.

DIRECTION 6 · YOUR PICK · 4 + 5

Composed answers on the carousel, with a living glass orb Your pick · approved Nov 11

Each composed answer is one glass panel on carousel A. The orb keeps the crystal glass you approved in September and gets a tesla-coil plasma inside it: a large white-hot core, never less than a third of the orb across, throws branching arcs to the glass, in your teal when you talk and Mortimer's rust when it does. Louder voice means more and thicker arcs, and thinking swirls them. The arcs move in depth: many fly toward you and strike the front of the glass with a bright contact spot, growing wider as they come, while the ones behind the core are dimmer and partly hidden. Touch the glass and an arc strikes that spot, like a plasma globe. The orb below is live: move over it, click it, and try the states under the frames.

Locked as the spec on Nov 11: you approved this orb as drawn below (the demo's energy code, crystal glass, depth, thicker arcs on small orbs, a core at least a third of the orb, tap and hold). Designed on Opus, then reviewed by Fable 5.2 on Nov 11; its corrections and changes are folded in below (states, hover/tap/hold, the "Needs you" overlay, the colour set).

Listening: four indigo arcs, ready for you.

Move your pointer or finger over the orb and an arc strikes the front of the glass where you touch. Click or tap mutes and unmutes; press and hold shows “You” while held, as in the demo. In the app those would drive the real mic and push-to-talk. The voice levels here are simulated; in the app they come from the measured mic and speech-output levels. Browser sketch, not the Swift renderer.

Depth for the orb itself: three options to pick from

Each is the approved orb, unchanged, with one depth treatment added, on the same sample rail so only the treatment differs. They follow the state buttons above. Untick "Depth on" to see the orb as approved. The crystal glass stays as it is in all three; none brings back a frosted rim.

Speed (10% budget)
1: two extra fills a frame, outside the glass; expected to fit. 2: one gradient and two offsets a frame; expected to fit, plus motion readings on the phone. 3: re-draws or samples the backdrop across the whole orb every frame; the most likely to break the 10% budget over the crystal shell (box 810, ROADMAP.md:810). SwiftUI Canvas cannot see the views behind it, so 3 needs either the rail drawn again inside the orb or a layerEffect shader, and nothing in the app uses Metal today. None of this is measured; the arcs probe in OrbShellFrameTimeTests would settle it.
Flash safety
1: the cast light follows the orb's capped brightness, so it changes no faster than the orb. 2: changes position, not brightness. 3: the far-wall echo is a fixed share of the same capped frame, so it rides the same cap; the refracted rail is steady, but text scrolling behind the orb would move through it magnified, which may distract. Checked by reasoning on the code, not on pixels.
Combining
1 and 2 stack without conflict: shadow below, tilt inside. 3 can sit with either but costs the most. Reduce Motion stills 2; Reduce Transparency would turn 3 off.

Already built
The orb already follows your real voice and Mortimer's. VoiceWaveView.swift:338-344 turns the measured mic and speech-output levels into two energies, and each channel brightens with its own level (CometOrbRenderer.swift:152-156) in teal and rust (AppTuning.swift:125-126; the comment at :122 still says amber). A "Thinking" label and dot pulse exist (OrbFieldView.swift:328, 378), but the orb itself only changes tint (CometOrbRenderer.swift:40). Reduce Motion stills it (VoiceWaveView.swift:340-341).
Seven states, drawn
The app has seven: offline, connecting, listening, you, Mortimer, thinking, muted (VoicePresentationState.swift:7). Muted looks the same as listening on the orb today (CometOrbRenderer.swift:34, 41), and it is push-to-talk's resting state. Proposed: listening is three slow indigo arcs; muted dims the core, keeps one faint arc and shows a mic-slash, since one arc alone would read as listening; offline is dark glass with no arcs; connecting is a grey sweep on the rim. "You over Mortimer" keeps a teal bloom while rust stays at its measured level, since your voice takes priority (VoicePresentationState.swift:67).
Hover, tap, hold
Tap and hold ship from the first version, not hover alone (your call). Mac: hover pulls an arc to the pointer, click toggles the mic like the mic button (MicControlsView.swift:34), press and hold is push-to-talk. One trap to fix first: releasing Space always mutes (MicControlsView.swift:113), so a held orb must share the pttOwned guard (MicControlsView.swift:19, 105-110) and restore the mic to how it was. Phone: touching and dragging pulls the arcs, tap toggles, hold talks, so the orb replaces a separate talk button. A working orb needs a button role and label; today it ignores clicks (VoiceWaveView.swift:63, OrbFieldView.swift:340).
"Needs you" is an overlay
On the orb, attention means a pending draft (OrbFieldView.swift:289), and that is only prepare_commit, prepare_push or repo_write_file (DisplayResultStore.swift:29-33). It is independent of who is talking, so the yellow ring sits outside the glass in any state. A file-send approval is a separate path (attachments.pendingOffer) that should drive the same ring.
Phone today, and the order
A preview on synthetic data: it "does not authenticate, contact a service, access audio" (PhonePreviewRootView.swift:159), with results as title and body text (:87-88). Its protocol carries voice offer, update and end, result select, pin, unpin, close and reopen, and call state (PhoneRemoteProtocolV1.swift:3-18), with no voice state and no approvals. Order: swipeable cards with a state-only orb now; a live orb after WS-30 voice, plus thinking, speaking and muted states in the protocol; composed cards once a block schema exists; the approval strip once an approval command exists.
Colours
Amber is the app's one attention token (AppTheme.swift:22-27), used in 52 places, so the change is one value. Connecting also uses amber (OrbFieldView.swift:368) and goes neutral. The table above is the set after review: it also moves listening and OK, because error and OK green would collide under deuteranopia (ΔE 7.5) and your teal sits beside OK green on the rail (OrbFieldView.swift:367, MicControlsView.swift:37).
Glass and plasma in the app
The app's crystal shell already draws in the right order: glass body, then the interior clipped to 0.885 of the radius, then the wall glow, wall line and window reflections (CometOrbRenderer.swift:290-335). Only the plasma() call at :300 changes; CrystalGlassRig.swift, whose values are frozen with your approval, stays as it is. SwiftUI Canvas is enough on the evidence so far: nothing in the app uses Metal, and .glassEffect is only used for rectangles (Glass.swift:54), so it cannot supply the sphere's reflections. Proposed: drop the orbiting comets (:286, :345), since the arcs carry the voice. The phone has no orb or Canvas yet; the same code should run there (untested).
Flash safety
The arcs are the demo's own code, unchanged, and re-strike every frame as they do there. What is limited is the orb's overall brightness: after each frame is drawn, its average light is measured and held inside a narrow band that drifts at most 4% a frame and turns round at most every 220 ms. When a frame would jump outside the band, all of it is dimmed evenly, or blended with the frame before, so the glow passes are never clipped. On a brightness model (not pixels), the demo as written gives about 14 flashes a second with 47% jumps, which fails the 3-a-second rule; with the cap it is at most 2.5 a second and a 10.6% largest step. About a third of frames get adjusted, by about 14% on average.
Small orbs and the core
Arc widths no longer shrink in step with the orb. At the demo's size (the big orb below) nothing changes. On smaller orbs widths shrink with the square root of the size, and the quietest arc's bright line never goes under 1.5 px, so its glow is at least 9 px. The contact spots follow the same rule. Quiet to loud, the bright line on the Mac rail orb goes from 0.5–1.2 px to 1.5–2.5 px, and on the phone orb from 0.5–1.1 px to 1.5–2.4 px; the demo's is 1.0–2.4 px. The core's visible edge, where its colour falls to half strength, is at least a third of the orb's width in every state, even silent: the quiet core grew from about a quarter to a third, and the voice still swells it by the demo's amount.
Accessibility
Reduce Motion: the arcs hold fixed shapes, brightness shows who is talking and changes over half a second or more, only the nearest arc moves to your pointer, and thinking gets a still concentric ring, since the swirl is the only thing telling it apart from Mortimer speaking. Reduce Transparency: the shell becomes an opaque disc through the existing Glass.swift fallbacks. The state stays in words beside the orb, so colour is never the only cue.
Effort · risk
Orb on the Mac: S to M, about 2 to 3 weeks, reusing the level plumbing. Composed answers need a layout field designed first: DisplayPayload has none (JarvisKit/AppMessage.swift:244-269). Performance gate: add an arcs probe to OrbShellFrameTimeTests (same 1440 × 220, 300 frames) and count arcs p50 or p95 more than 10% above the crystal shell's as a regression, as box 810 does (ROADMAP.md:810); the shell's p95 is 14.15 ms against a 16.7 ms frame. Caps: 14 arcs, 12 segments and 2 forks each, glow from wide faint strokes rather than blurs. The idle rate is 15 frames a second (VoiceWaveView.swift:72), slower than the arcs change. Test changes need your OK: the idle colour in code is still #7C8CFF and two tests expect a coloured nucleus, which is now white (VoiceWaveRenderingTests.swift:297, 314). Untested: the real frame time.

Why this combination works

Composed Answers fixes what an answer looks like, the carousel gives a stack of them a place to sit and turn, and the orb makes the voice side feel alive by showing real signals: who is talking, how loud, and when Mortimer is working. Today only a small label and a dot pulse show Mortimer is working; the thinking swirl puts that on the orb itself.

What this rests on

Untested until run on the Mac

  • Why the second-screen buttons render white: check the supporting window in Dark and Light appearance, focused and unfocused.
  • Glass legibility over real wallpapers and the cost of a blurred live orb; only flat-background test renders exist.
  • Space under Full Keyboard Access, and how VoiceOver reads the glyph buttons.
  • The drawer tabs, weather, radar, atlas, roadmap and workflow views were judged from code only; no screenshots of them are in the repo.
  • The orb sketch: how the arcs look in SwiftUI Canvas, their frame time on the Mac and an iPhone, and how touch feels on the phone. Flash safety was checked on a brightness model, not on rendered pixels, and the sketch could not be rendered off-screen here to compare it frame by frame with the demo, and the browser port of the crystal glass was not compared with the app's renders on screen. The voice levels in the sketch are simulated.
  • The three depth options: how each looks in SwiftUI, its frame time against the 10% budget, and whether phone tilt feels right for option 2 (here it follows the pointer). Option 3's refraction is approximated with 18 rings of scaled backdrop, not true optics.
  • The colour numbers are calculations against the app background; contrast on glass panels and real users with colour-vision differences are not tested.
  • All mockup content is sample data, and the mockups are HTML approximations of SwiftUI, not renders of the app.
