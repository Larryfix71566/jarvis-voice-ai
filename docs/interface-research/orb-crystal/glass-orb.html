Why the current shell reads as light, not glass

  1. The sphere fill runs 2.5% → 6% opacity from center to 65% radius. Without body, the eye sees a ring drawn around the plasma rather than an object holding it.
  2. The only highlight is a disc 0.25 × radius wide at 40% peak opacity, then blurred. Glass speculars are small, sharp-edged and close to full white.
  3. The rim band, arcs and highlight are all multiplied by strength, which drops to 0.16 in standby. A physical globe keeps its reflections when the light inside turns off; this one mostly vanishes.
  4. Edge brightness comes from one ice band at 97.5% radius. Real glass reflectance climbs smoothly toward the silhouette: about 4% face-on, about 50% at 99% radius, 100% at the edge.
  5. The back half of each comet orbit is drawn under a sphere fill that stays at or below 6% across the inner two-thirds, so it shows through at nearly full brightness. Nothing tells the eye there is glass in the way.

What changes in the Swift

  • Only the shell part of CometOrbRenderer.draw changes: the halo, sphere fill, broken arcs and reflection block. plasma(…), comets(…), AtomMotion and the color tokens are untouched.
  • A small static rig computes the light outlines once per radius from the mirror-sphere mapping (surface normal = normalize(light + view)). Option C adds the horizon outline the same way.
  • Every primitive used here exists on GraphicsContext: radial, linear and conic gradients, clip(to:options: .inverse), even-odd fills, .blur, and the .screen, .plusLighter and .destinationIn blend modes.
  • The existing rendering tests check the nucleus hue at the center pixel, teal and orange pixel counts, and stillness under Reduced Motion and in standby. The options keep every highlight off-center and all motion on the shared phase, so they should pass. [untested until swift test runs on the Mac]
  • Reflections do not depend on voice state, so they can be drawn once per size and reused; that keeps every option at the current blur count per frame. Frame time on the Mac is not measured yet. [untested]

Preview renderer: a JavaScript port of CometOrbRenderer.swift. The plasma, comets and current shell follow the Swift line for line, except that comet bloom is merged into one blur layer per pass for browser speed. Voice levels are a simulated speech envelope passed through the app's 40 ms attack / 180 ms release smoothing and its 0.55 input and 0.42 output gains. Canvas and SwiftUI interpolate gradients and size blurs slightly differently, so the Swift build is the final word on the look.
